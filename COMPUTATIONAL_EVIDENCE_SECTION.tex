\section{Computational evidence}\label{sec:computational-evidence}

The state-complexity characterization is exact and is proved analytically.  To expose the finite-size behavior and guard against state/bit conversion errors, the accompanying script \texttt{audit/computational\_evidence.py} evaluates the theorem on a finite range of network sizes.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{c|rrrrr}
$n$ & 4 & 8 & 16 & 32 & 64 \\
\hline
required active interval $L=n-1$ & 3 & 7 & 15 & 31 & 63 \\
exact states $S=n+1$ & 5 & 9 & 17 & 33 & 65 \\
persistent bits $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7
\end{tabular}
\caption{Finite evaluation of the deterministic locally timed BTAI state complexity.}
\label{tab:local-timer-finite}
\end{table}

The script checks monotonicity, the identity $S=L+2$, and the exact ceiling conversion from states to persistent bits.  These computations do not extend the theorem to unrestricted deterministic non-idle-start broadcast and do not imply an exact randomized minimum; they are a reproducible finite audit of the stated class result.
